01 / The brief

Design for a better tomorrow.

The Design ArCHIVE — Student Architecture Awards 2026 recognises thoughtful architectural work that demonstrates design intent, contextual understanding, spatial quality, technical resolution and responsibility toward people and the environment.

The theme invites projects that respond critically to contemporary needs and imagine more inclusive, resilient and meaningful built environments. Projects may explore social and cultural context, environmental performance, emerging technologies, new ways of living and working, public life, adaptive reuse and urban relationships.

04 / Eligibility and entry

Who can enter?

  • Architecture students and recent architecture graduates.
  • Each entry must be submitted by an individual participant; group and team entries are not eligible.
  • The submitted project must be the participant’s own work, with collaboration and source material acknowledged.
  • Academic studio projects, thesis projects and other eligible architectural projects may be submitted if the participant has the right to submit and publish them.

The recommended recent-graduate window is graduation within the previous 24 months; the organiser must confirm this before public launch.

07 / Submission requirements

Present your project clearly.

Each entry must include 10–12 A1 sheets. Recommended content includes:

  • Project title, category and concise design statement.
  • Site context, site analysis, concept development and research.
  • Plans, sections, elevations, circulation, user experience and accessibility.
  • Material, structural and construction thinking appropriate to the project stage.
  • Climate response, environmental strategies, diagrams, models, details and key views.

Anonymous submission: use only the unique entry code on the sheets. Do not include participant names, college names, logos, signatures, watermarks or other identifying details. Final file formats, upload method and size limits will be announced before submissions open.

08 / Selection process

Three jury stages.

JURY 01

Initial shortlisting

Closed, category-wise review of eligible entries. Top 20 projects selected in each category.

JURY 02

Online review

The Top 20 in each category are evaluated from submitted sheets. Top 7 are selected and invited to present.

JURY 03

Final presentation

Online or hybrid presentation and jury discussion by the Top 7 in each category. Winners, runners-up and five special mentions are announced.

09 / Judging criteria

What the jury evaluates.

20% · Concept

Clarity of the central idea, problem framing and consistency of intent.

20% · Resolution

Spatial organisation, design development and architectural resolution.

15% · Context

Response to site, climate, culture, urban conditions and local needs.

15% · Experience

Programme fit, circulation, usability, inclusion and accessibility.

15% · Responsibility

Climate awareness, resource sensitivity, resilience and social value.

10% Innovation · 5% Communication

Original thinking, experimentation, feasibility and clear visual communication.

11 / Frequently asked questions

Before you prepare your entry.

The final competition brief and registration announcement will confirm any details still being finalised.

Who is eligible to enter?

Architecture students and recent architecture graduates may enter. The recommended recent-graduate window is 24 months, but the organiser has not yet confirmed the final eligibility period. Check the published brief before registering.

Can I submit as a team?

No. Entries must be submitted by an individual participant; group and team entries are not eligible.

Which projects and categories can I enter?

Academic studio projects, thesis projects and other eligible architectural projects may be submitted when you have the right to submit and publish the work. Choose one of the five categories and acknowledge any collaboration or source material.

How many sheets should I prepare?

Prepare 10–12 A1 sheets explaining your concept, context, development, architectural resolution and environmental or social response. The final file format, upload method and size limits will be announced with the submission instructions.

Will the jury review entries anonymously?

Yes. Use your assigned entry code and remove your name, college identifiers, logos, signatures, watermarks and other identifying details from the sheets. Instructions for receiving the code will be provided before submissions open.

What are the entry fees?

The planned fee is ₹299 for Early Bird and ₹499 for Late Entry. Dates and final fee terms have not been announced yet.

How are entries shortlisted?

The planned process has three jury stages: an initial review selecting the Top 20 in each category, an online review selecting the Top 7, and a final presentation stage for awards and special mentions. Presentation details and dates will be shared in the final brief.

When does registration open, and where do I submit?

Registration dates, the official registration link and submission instructions are still to be announced. This website does not accept entries or payments; please wait for Squaroid’s official announcement before paying or sending files.

May I submit a project created with others?

Only individual entries are accepted. Your submitted work must be your own, and any collaboration or source material must be acknowledged. Make sure you have permission to submit and publish the project.
